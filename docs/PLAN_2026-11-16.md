# Development plan: 2026-11-16 feedback round

- **Sources:** owner feedback (chat, 2026-11-16) and Rowen's test notes ("RTS feedback: requested changes", 2026-11-16).
- **Mockup:** [`docs/design/hud_mockup_2026-11-16.png`](design/hud_mockup_2026-11-16.png).
- **Rule:** Rowen's notes are treated as top priority. Where Rowen and the owner disagree, the owner decides; where neither says, follow the mockup when it fits the game, otherwise use judgement and note why.

## 0. How this round runs

- **Waves.**
  - Wave 1 works in parallel across 11 workstreams (A–K).
  - Then integration and review by the lead: a whole-project compile and the server tests.
  - Then Wave 2: fixes found in review, plus **balance, visual and performance testing**. Wave 2 uses the Unity editor one agent at a time.
- **Ownership.** Each workstream owns the files listed for it. When a file is shared (the most common being `server/sim/actionDefinitions.js`, `actionGame.js`, `Commander*.cs`, `GameBootstrap.cs`, `UnitRenderer.cs`):
  - edit only your own section;
  - re-read the file right before each edit, and use small, exact edits;
  - never rewrite or reformat a whole shared file.
- **Editor.** Only workstream **A** (performance) may drive the Unity editor in Wave 1, through the Unity MCP. Everyone else type-checks C# with the dotnet project (copy `scratchpad/impl2check/check.csproj` into your own scratch folder) and runs `npm test` for server work. A known failure that is not yours: "action saves continue deterministic spatial simulation".
- **Hard rules.** No git commands that change the tree. No deploys. Workshop (Asset Store) media stays git-ignored. Write C# 9 that compiles: check every identifier with grep, keep braces balanced, avoid CS0136.
- **Every agent finishes by:**
  1. compile-checking with 0 errors;
  2. running `npm test` if it touched the server;
  3. appending a section to `docs/CHANGELOG_2026-11-16.md` (create it if missing; one section per workstream) covering what changed, what is verified, what isn't, and the protocol changes others need;
  4. reporting back.

## 1. Priorities (Rowen's order, which we follow)

1. Building placement exploit: build only in your own territory.
2. Fog of war missing.
3. Lag at mid zoom. **Target: 30 FPS or more at mid zoom on the test machine, and things never disappear when zoomed out.**
4. Pathfinding: groups, large units, base interiors, roads over rivers.
5. Attack move and camera keys.
6. HUD and radial menu rework, per the mockup.
7. Base structure and visuals.
8. Everything else.

## 2. Workstreams

### A. Performance, LOD, fog of war, camera rendering (has the Unity editor in Wave 1)
**Owns:**
- `Runtime/Rendering/*` (except `SoldierRig`, `MilitaryMeshes`)
- `Runtime/Terrain/*` (except the crater code in `ChunkMeshJob`)
- `Runtime/Effects/Atmosphere.cs`, `Effects/Wildlife.cs`
- `Shroud.shader`, `SECommon.hlsl` fog functions, `InstancedLit.shader`
- the LOD and culling sections of `CityRenderer.cs` (the `Draw` path only), `Settings/*.asset`, `ProjectSettings/QualitySettings`/`GraphicsSettings`
- the draw-level selection in `UnitRenderer` (`level` / `close` thresholds only)

**Tasks:**
- **Profile first.** Measure with the Unity Profiler (`manage_profiler` / Profiler API through `execute_code`) at close, mid and strategic zoom on a large map with armies, and record baseline FPS and the top CPU and GPU costs. Fix the biggest:
  - draw calls and instance counts, and per-frame CPU work in the renderers;
  - shadow casters and shadow distance (shadows at mid zoom are a likely cost);
  - tree density and polygon counts: cut tree quality and density first, as Rowen asks;
  - the shroud march cost: half resolution if needed;
  - the terrain shader's extra samples;
  - the URP render scale and MSAA.
- **LOD and disappearing objects.**
  - Widen the LOD distances so transitions don't happen across a narrow band.
  - **Nothing may stop existing when zoomed out.** Small units, buildings, trees and defenses must always draw at least a cheap impostor, dot or badge. Rowen: "Always draw something, even a few dots."
  - Check the camera far clip and the culling bounds.
- **Fog of war.**
  - Rowen reports "the whole map is visible". Find out why: the explored-but-unseen dimming, the visibility texture, or the server sending all units.
  - Unseen areas must be darker and must hide enemy units and buildings that aren't currently seen. Undiscovered areas stay under the cloud bank.
  - Every unit, building and base has a line of sight; verify this server-side together with C.
- **Camera.**
  - Use a perspective camera with a small field of view, about 10–30% perspective that varies with zoom, instead of orthographic. Re-tune the shroud for it (it already supports both).
  - Cap zoom-in further out than now.
  - Alt + mouse rotates the camera (with H).
- **Birds and ambient life** freeze while paused.
- **Black patches.** Craters and some debris draw as flat black blobs, and many roofs are near-black. Fix the materials and shader inputs; coordinate with I (roofs) and J (debris).
- **Acceptance:** at least 30 FPS at mid zoom with two armies of about 60 units each, with numbers recorded in the changelog; no pop-out; fog of war working.

### B. Server: placement, territory, engineers, bases, economy, upgrades
**Owns:**
- the placement, territory, construction and economy sections of `server/sim/actionBases.js`, `actionEconomy.js`, `actionGame.js`
- the building and upgrade definitions in `actionDefinitions.js`
- engineer AI (new file)
- related tests

**Tasks:**
- **Build only in your own territory.** Everything placed from the build wheel (defenses, field works, roads, trenches) must be inside the builder's territory. Never on enemy land. Lost territory keeps what is already built but can't be built in. This closes the fortress-spam exploit.
- **Defenses claim territory.** Trenches, towers, bunkers and the fortress/outpost claim nearby hexes (radius 1).
- **Automatic engineers.**
  - Remove player-trained engineers from the action ruleset.
  - Each base gets its own AI engineers: 2 / 3 / 4 by era.
  - They walk out and build the improvements you place nearby; you have no direct control.
  - They can be killed, and lost engineers respawn for free over time.
  - The AI must keep working with this.
- **New base buildable.** It costs 1000, can go anywhere in your territory and must connect to a road; completing it creates a real base site.
- **Base slots** by tier: 3 / 5 / 7 (max 7).
- **Era upgrades.** Each era unlocks one more upgrade level for production, power or supply buildings, bought on the individual building.
- **Defenses and perimeter** can be upgraded. City defenses at upgrade level give the city **built-in AA**.
- **Smaller footprints** for sandbags, roads, trenches and watchtowers, so they can be placed more densely. The fortress becomes an "outpost" that claims territory.
- **Functional upgrades.** Replace flat % buffs with real changes. For example:
  - grenades: an actual area burst against troops in cover;
  - rocket teams: a squad member fires an AT rocket;
  - body armor: a soldier survives one extra lethal hit;
  - marksmen: a long-range scoped shot at officers or crews;
  - field engineering: field repair in combat;
  - heavy armor: a damage threshold that ignores small arms;
  - improved logistics: faster reinforcement walking from bases;
  - networked command: shared vision radius between units.

  Each must change behaviour and be visible to J and D as an equipment or flag bit. Document it.
- **Income smoothing:** accrue resources every tick (10 Hz), not once per second.
- **Rifle squad** gets more men (see J). Squad HP and damage scale with the man count, with casualties reducing the count. This is shared with E for balance numbers.

### C. Server: pathfinding, movement, orders, stances
**Owns:**
- `server/sim/actionNavigation.js`, `actionOrders.js`
- the movement and stance parts of `actionGame.js` and `actionCombat.js`
- related tests

**Tasks:**
- **Group pathfinding.**
  - Groups and large units get stuck on bad terrain and pick terrible routes.
  - Fix the flow fields and formation slots so they route around obstacles together.
  - Large units must not get stuck inside bases: friendly bases are passable cleanly, so compound collision must leave lanes.
  - Inside a friendly zone of control, relax the rules so units slide past each other.
- **Tanks on roads over rivers.** A road crossing a river (a bridge) is a valid route for every vehicle.
- **Unit speed consistency.** Units sometimes move at very different speeds; find out why (terrain multipliers, catch-up logic, formation speed matching). Keep each unit type's speed consistent and have groups move at the slowest member's speed. **Slow all units by about 30%** (Rowen asks for 20–50%).
- **Stances:** aggressive, defensive and hold fire, with hold position as before. Server behaviour plus a command `{c:'stance', units, stance}`.
- **Attack move** as a first-class order, `{c:'move', attackMove:true}` (already exists). Verify it behaves like a normal RTS attack move: engage on the way, then continue.
- **Rally points** take an action: move or attack-move.
- **Impassable feedback.** Provide a cheap "can these units reach here" query (or terrain flags on the client) so the client can show a crossed-out cursor.
- **Speed buttons in spectate.** They do nothing while watching an AI match; fix the observer speed path (`worker.js` observer command).

### D. Air operations, fuel, AA (server and client air visuals)
**Owns:**
- `server/sim/airOperations.js`, `airfields.js`, `airPlanner.js`
- the aircraft and AA parts of `actionCombat.js` and `actionDefinitions.js` (air units)
- in `UnitRenderer`, only the air functions (`Fly`, `FlySnapshot`, `AddAircraft`)
- the airburst effect in `EffectsRenderer` (new partial `EffectsRenderer.Air.cs`)

**Tasks:**
- **Fuel.**
  - A pseudo-fuel system: aircraft have endurance (fighters about 60 s, bombers about 90 s, helicopters about 120 s, drones less).
  - When fuel runs low they automatically return to the nearest friendly airfield or base, refuel there and resume their mission or patrol.
  - Not strict: they never explode. If they can't reach a base they still return; out of fuel, they slow to minimum speed and can't attack.
  - Fuel limits range: patrol points too far from a base are clamped or refused with feedback.
  - Show fuel on the selection card and hover card: a status field in the snapshot (fuel 0–255).
- **Patrol routes.** Easy to set: with aircraft selected, a patrol command with several waypoints (Shift-click waypoints, or P then clicks).
- **Flight paths.**
  - Planes fly long flight paths spanning several hexes: wide racetrack orbits and strafing runs, no hovering.
  - Helicopters patrol in a loose circuit of about 2–3 hexes around their point instead of hovering in place.
- **AA airbursts.**
  - AA guns and AA batteries fire visible flak airbursts: puffs of black smoke at altitude, with a flash and shrapnel streaks.
  - Server events carry the burst positions. Client effects must be visible from mid zoom.
- **City AA.** Cities with upgraded defenses have built-in AA (the trigger is from B; the firing logic is here).

### E. Maps and balance
**Owns:**
- `server/sim/authoredMaps.js`, `mapCatalog.js`
- the map parts of `actionMap.js`
- unit costs and stats in `actionDefinitions.js`, in coordination with B
- balance tests and bench scripts

**Tasks:**
- **Maps.** Better, more fun, more strategic maps, and **not too big**:
  - 1v1 around 72–88;
  - 2v2 around 96–110;
  - 3v3 around 120–136;
  - FFA for 3 and 4 players.

  At least 2 per format. Each needs clear lanes, chokepoints, flank routes, an expansion economy, central objectives, water features with crossings, and room for a desolate no-man's-land between the lines.
  - Re-tune the existing three if needed.
  - Every map's catalog entry carries a **preview**: a small top-down grid (for example 64×64 bytes of terrain class, plus start positions, bases and outposts) for the menu to draw.
  - Every map must be selectable (all of them listed, filtered by player count).
- **Balance pass.**
  - Troops are a little too expensive; aim for more units on the field and epic battles.
  - Use AI-vs-AI sims across the maps (at least 3 seeds per map) and log army sizes over time, kills, time to first contact, match length and unit-type win rates. Use mirror tests: equal-cost compositions should roughly trade.
  - Targets:
    - first contact 3–6 minutes;
    - typical army size 40–80 per player by minute 12;
    - matches 18–30 minutes;
    - a no-man's-land forms between fronts (trenches and craters).
  - Cut unit costs about 15–25% where the data supports it, and adjust income. Write the numbers into the changelog.
- **Rifle squad size.** Agree with B and J (for example, 5 visible men up to 8, HP per man).

### F. HUD per the mockup (docked console, minimap, selection, hover, markers)
**Owns:**
- `Runtime/UI/Hud.Shell.cs`, `Hud.cs` (shell parts), `Minimap.cs`, `Hud.Overlay.cs`, `Hud.Command.cs`, `Hud.Action.cs` (selection card and markers), `Hud.Tooltips.cs`
- the `SE.uss` / `SE.Minimap.uss` / `SE.Command.uss` selection-card styles
- `Commander.cs` (drag box, move markers only)

**Tasks:**
- **Drop the top bar.** Only floating Pause and Menu buttons stay at the top right; pause and game speed can live in the menu. A match clock sits at top centre.
- **Minimap console** (bottom-left, docked):
  - slim Materials, Energy and Command bars with rates, attached to the minimap, small and subtle;
  - an Era hexagon badge;
  - an idle-units button (Rowen wants it back, on the minimap);
  - an attack warning banner ("CINDERHOLD UNDER ATTACK · Space to jump") plus a minimap ping, both on new attacks.
- **The minimap itself:**
  - fit the whole map to the circle by default;
  - optional zoom on Z/X, or Z as a toggle, **showing a smaller local section** (the owner wants a local view);
  - the camera view drawn as a trapezoid;
  - no clustering of unit dots into one blob: aggregate per cell with a count, or offset them;
  - attack warnings.
- **Bottom centre:** two docked buttons, Build (B, building glyph) and Terrain (T, hex glyph). They open the radial wheels (G).
- **Selection panel** (bottom-right, mockup):
  - "N units selected", an **Attack move · F** button, **Stance: X** and a condition bar;
  - a box per unit type with an icon, name and count;
  - below about 6 units, don't group: one box per unit.
- **Hover card:** hovering any unit (friendly or enemy) shows name, owner and team, a one-line role ("what it is weak against"), condition and stance.
- **Drag selection box** visible: a filled translucent rectangle with a bright border.
- **Move markers.** Each order shows one clean marker: green for move, red for attack-move. It fades after about 1.5 s and never sticks or duplicates (fixes "the green circle never goes away").
- **Unit visibility.** Troops must be easier to find on screen: team-coloured ground rings always visible at any distance, and an icon above enemy squads (with J).
- **Production view** near the minimap is optional. Low priority; at least support multi-base select with "all production" batch queueing (with G).

### G. Radial menus (base, build, terrain) per the mockup
**Owns:**
- `Runtime/UI/RadialMenu.cs`, `Hud.Base.cs`, `Hud.DefenseBar.cs` (to be replaced), `SE.Command.uss` wheel styles
- `Commander.Rings.cs` and `Commander.Action.cs` (build and placement parts)

**Tasks:**
- **Shape.**
  - 9 segments on every wheel (odd, so there is a clear top), with no gaps between the segments and the centre.
  - The centre circle is enlarged and shows the highlighted item's **name, description and cost**. No box below, nothing covering icons.
  - The top segment is building upgrades.
  - A "next page" segment appears only when a second page is needed.
  - Readable over busy bases: a more opaque backing (about 85%) with a subtle blur-free shadow; no label overlap.
- **Production.**
  - Unit icons show name and cost through the centre.
  - Progress is a green ring that fills around the item, with the queued count at the bottom of the item.
- **Base wheel.**
  - Slots follow the tier: 3 / 5 / 7.
  - Perimeter sits near the bottom.
  - Defenses are no longer on the base wheel.
  - Era upgrades per building (B).
- **Build wheel (B key or button)** places all defenses and improvements: trench, road, sandbags, watchtower, bunker, AT, AA, outpost, new base (1000). Placement is only valid in your own territory; show the reason when it isn't.
- **Terrain wheel (T key or button):** pay to deform terrain in your own territory (raise, lower, flatten, smooth). This needs a server command from B, or do it with the base engineers.
- **Remove the old bottom defense bar**, since the Build button replaces it.
- **Base UI** must not cover the defensive buildings or anything else at the bottom. Position wheels so they never overlap the bottom-centre buttons, the selection panel or the minimap console.
- **Batch production.** With several bases (or production buildings) selected, "all production" queues units across them.

### H. Controls and camera input
**Owns:** `Runtime/Input/InputFacade.cs`, `Runtime/Camera/RtsCamera.cs` (input only; A owns projection and rendering), and the input/order parts of `Commander.cs` and `Commander.Action.cs`.

**Tasks:**
- **Camera keys.** Pan moves to the arrow keys (and screen edges). WASD and QE are freed for commands. Alt + mouse drag rotates.
- **Attack move.** With units selected, **F** then click issues an attack move with a red marker. It's also on the selection panel button. Offer it for all units and for local units (for example, double-tap F for every combat unit on screen).
- **Stances** (cycle with V, plus the selection-panel button) send `{c:'stance'}` (C).
- **Rally points** take an action: right-click is move, F-click is attack-move.
- **Crossed-out cursor** when an order would target impassable terrain.
- **Remove the C-then-2 flow.** Unit wheels stay available but are not needed for basic orders.
- Update the field guide hotkey text (`FieldGuide.cs`) and the settings key-binding list.

  - They should be **larger and readable** (turrets especially) and upgradable visually by level.

- **Damage shows visually** on buildings, with no health bar: cracks, scorching, missing chunks, smoke and fire by damage stage.
- **Territory outline is hexagonal** (it follows the hex edges) in team colours, and stays subtle.
- **Black roofs and patches** get proper materials.
- **Improvements connect** visually to their base hub with small paths.

## 3. Review, integration and testing (lead, after Wave 1)

1. Compile the whole project in Unity (Runtime, Steam, Editor), run `npm test` and resolve cross-stream conflicts.
2. **Performance test:** a scripted camera sweep (close, mid, strategic) on the largest map with armies. Record FPS (average and 1 % low) against the 30 FPS target, before and after.
3. **Visual test:** screenshots of the main menu, lobby, HUD, wheels, base at tiers 1–3, defenses, AA airburst, aircraft orbits, trenches with troops, fog of war, and zoom levels with no pop-out.
4. **Balance test:** AI-vs-AI sims on every map, with the metrics tables in the changelog.
5. Wave 2 agents fix what review finds. Then the final build, commit and publish, only with the owner's go-ahead.
